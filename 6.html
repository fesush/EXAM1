Part 1 - Lecture 1

The Google Effect: Digital Amnesia

Eğitim ve Bilişsel Psikoloji | Sınav Süresi Metni 2 Defa Dinletilir

Okuma Hızı:
Dinlemeyi Başlat
Dinleme Hakkı: 1 / 2
LECTURE SCRIPT (Part 1):

Welcome to today's lecture on cognitive psychology. Today, we will examine how our reliance on the internet, specifically search engines and social media, is altering human memory. Psychologists refer to this phenomenon as 'digital amnesia' or the 'Google effect.' Digital amnesia is defined as the tendency to quickly forget information that we know can be easily accessed online.

In a landmark study, researchers asked participants to type trivia facts into a computer. Half of the participants were told their data would be saved, and the other half were told it would be erased permanently. The results were striking. The group that believed the information was saved made much less effort to remember the facts themselves. Instead of remembering the actual information, their brains prioritized remembering where to find the information—in this case, the specific folder on the computer where the data was stored.

This cognitive shift has major implications for modern education. True learning requires transferring information from our short-term working memory into our long-term memory. However, constant scrolling and relying on digital devices creates a state of 'superficial processing.' Because we do not engage deeply with the material, the critical transfer of knowledge to long-term memory rarely happens.

Despite this alarming trend, some neurologists argue that the Google effect isn't necessarily destroying our brains. Instead, they suggest our brains are simply adapting to act as an efficient index. By offloading the storage of raw, basic facts to the internet, we are theoretically freeing up our cognitive space for more complex problem-solving and critical thinking.

Note-Taking Sheet

Araştırma deneyini ve eğitim üzerindeki etkilerini not alın:

Note-Taking Questions

1. How does the speaker define "digital amnesia"?

2. In the computer experiment, what did the participants whose data was saved prioritize remembering?

3. According to the lecture, what is necessary for true learning to occur?

4. What is the consequence of the "superficial processing" caused by digital devices?

5. What positive view do some neurologists hold regarding the Google effect?

Part 2 - Lecture 2

The Deceptive Practice of "Greenwashing"

İş Ahlakı (Business Ethics) & Pazarlama | While-Listening Mode

Okuma Hızı:
Dinlemeyi Başlat
Dinleme Hakkı: 1 / 2
LECTURE SCRIPT (Part 2):

Good morning. In our business ethics course today, we are going to tackle a deceptive marketing practice known as 'greenwashing.' Greenwashing occurs when a company spends more time and money marketing itself as environmentally friendly than actually minimizing its environmental impact. It is essentially a public relations stunt designed to capitalize on the growing consumer demand for sustainable products.

One of the most common greenwashing tactics is the use of vague terminology. Companies frequently plaster words like 'all-natural,' 'earth-friendly,' or 'eco-safe' on their packaging. The problem is that these terms are largely unregulated by the government. Because they have no strict legal definition, any company can use them without facing legal penalties.

Another tactic is the use of misleading visual imagery. Simply changing a product's packaging to feature green leaves, earth tones, or pictures of happy animals can trick the consumer's subconscious into believing the product is organic or sustainable, even if it is filled with harsh chemicals.

A more complex strategy is called the 'hidden trade-off.' This happens when a brand highlights one single environmentally positive attribute while completely ignoring the severe ecological damage caused by the rest of its production process. For example, a fast-food chain might proudly announce they have switched to paper straws, while completely ignoring the massive deforestation and carbon emissions caused by their beef supply chain.

Ultimately, the greatest danger of greenwashing is that it creates widespread consumer skepticism. When people realize they have been deceived, they lose trust in all eco-friendly claims. This financially harms the genuinely sustainable companies that are trying to make a real difference, as consumers can no longer tell who is telling the truth.

While-Listening Questions

1. How is "greenwashing" defined in the lecture?

2. Why are terms like "all-natural" or "eco-safe" considered problematic?

3. How do companies use misleading visual imagery to trick consumers?

4. What describes the "hidden trade-off" strategy used by some brands?

5. What is mentioned as the greatest danger or ultimate consequence of greenwashing?
